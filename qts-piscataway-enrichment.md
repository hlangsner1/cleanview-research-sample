## Local Permitting Precedent (Analogous Case)

QTS has a documented pattern of local planning board friction when proposing second buildings at existing NJ campuses. At its **East Windsor Township** site (different Middlesex-area location, same company, same "second building at an existing campus" structure):

- The Planning Board **postponed its vote** after a packed hearing where public testimony could not be completed in one session (pushed to a third meeting).
- QTS offered **$2M/year in projected tax revenue** and a **$300K contribution to the township's Affordable Housing Trust Fund** as part of its case for approval.
- Primary reported concern from residents: potential **utility bill increases** for existing ratepayers.

*Source: DataCenterDynamics reporting on East Windsor Township Planning Board proceedings (TAPinto local coverage cited).* This is not the Piscataway project itself, but is a directly relevant precedent for how the same developer's expansion proposals have played out locally in this market — worth pulling actual Piscataway/Middlesex County planning board agendas directly to confirm whether a comparable hearing is scheduled.

## Financing Context (Greenbond Context Note)

QTS announced in April 2026 that it was marketing a **$4.6B financing package**, beginning with a 10-year bond marketed as **investment-grade green debt** (Moody's Baa2 rating; arranged by JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo, and SMBC Nikko).

- **Stated use of proceeds** (per Moody's and Bloomberg reporting): refinancing credit facilities tied to QTS's Fayetteville, GA AI campus, and general corporate purposes — not a discrete incremental environmental project.
- **Green label basis**: QTS's Green Finance Framework, reviewed by Sustainalytics and aligned with ICMA Green Bond/Green Loan Principles, ties eligibility to building-level efficiency features (e.g., QTS's "Freedom" zero-water cooling design), rather than to a reduction in the facility's overall electricity demand.

**Why this matters for the enrichment record:** the gap between "green bond" as a financing label and "large net-new load facility" as the physical reality is a specific, factual point — not a characterization — that community opposition in future planning board hearings (for Piscataway or elsewhere) could reasonably raise, particularly in a state where the legislature is already advancing "large load" tariff bills aimed at protecting ratepayers from data center-driven cost increases. Flagging this as a live regulatory/community tension point relevant to project timeline and risk, not as a claim about QTS's intent.
